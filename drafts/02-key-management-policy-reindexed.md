# Web3 Cryptographic Key Management Policy

## 1. Purpose

**1.1** This Policy establishes mandatory organizational requirements for managing cryptographic keys and associated keying material used by the Company. It applies governance and control expectations across the complete key lifecycle so that each keying-material set has an accountable owner, an authorized purpose, and protection proportionate to its risk.

**1.2** The Company shall preserve confidentiality, integrity, availability, authenticity, authorized use, and accountability. Controls shall address each property relevant to the keying material and its authority, including cases in which loss, substitution, misuse, or unavailability creates greater risk than disclosure.

**1.3** The Company shall maintain controls intended to prevent unauthorized signing, asset movement, protocol control, service compromise, validator penalties, and loss of recoverability. It shall assess these outcomes when designing, approving, operating, and reviewing key-management controls, including controls over dependencies and recovery paths.

**1.4** The Company shall maintain a documented Cryptographic Key Management System (CKMS). The CKMS shall identify its governance, scope, control architecture, accountable roles, lifecycle arrangements, evidence, dependencies, and interfaces with incident response, business continuity, and third-party oversight.

**1.5** This Policy defines mandatory outcomes and governance. Processes define lifecycle flows, Standards define technical criteria, and Procedures and SOPs define approved actions and evidence. Subordinate documents shall be consistent with this Policy, assign implementation responsibilities, and provide sufficient detail for repeatable execution and independent assessment.

## 2. Scope

**2.1** This Policy applies to Company personnel, contractors, third parties, systems, services, environments, and activities that create, receive, derive, store, use, administer, recover, rotate, revoke, or destroy Company keying material. Business and system owners shall identify in-scope activities and dependencies, including automated operations and supporting administrative access, and shall not exclude them solely because custody or operation is delegated.

**2.2** In-scope material includes private and public keys, symmetric keys, certificates, key-encryption keys, seeds, mnemonics, HD wallet roots, derived private keys, and encrypted keystores. Scope applies according to the security or operational consequence of disclosure, modification, substitution, misuse, unavailability, or loss, rather than only to whether the material is secret.

**2.3** It also includes threshold or MPC shares, recovery material, and remote-signer credentials. Such material remains in scope when no single component independently provides signing or recovery authority, because combinations, dependencies, or administrative paths may enable reconstruction, use, or control.

**2.4** It also includes validator signing keys, withdrawal credentials, transaction and treasury keys, governance and smart-contract authorities, and service credentials used to access key-management services. The applicable Key Owner shall account for both the cryptographic object and the assets, protocol roles, services, or administrative capabilities that it can directly or indirectly control.

**2.5** Keying material managed by an approved external provider is in scope. The accountable Key Owner shall ensure that provider responsibilities, Company-retained responsibilities, assurance evidence, incident obligations, recovery arrangements, and exit requirements are documented and subject to Company oversight.

**2.6** This Policy applies to production and non-production environments. Production keying material shall not be used outside production unless a time-bound, documented exception establishes a legitimate need and equivalent safeguards. Non-production material shall be separately generated and identifiable, and its controls shall reflect the authority, data, connectivity, and production dependencies available in that environment.

## 3. Definitions

**3.1** **Associated keying material** means material that enables generation, derivation, protection, recovery, access, or use of a cryptographic key. The term covers material whose compromise, loss, or misuse could affect a key even when that material is not itself a cryptographic key.

**3.2** It includes seeds, mnemonics, shares, encrypted keystores, recovery credentials, and protected backups. This list is illustrative rather than exhaustive and includes combinations of components that can reconstruct, unlock, authorize, or otherwise enable use of a key.

**3.3** **Authorized signing context** means the immutable, approved set of data and constraints for a signature. It includes the final signing payload or digest, network or chain identifier, signer, purpose, authorization, and expiry. Where relevant to the operation, it also records or references replay controls, destination, asset, value, contract, function, and other restrictions needed to demonstrate that approval and signing covered the same action.

**3.4** **CKMS** means the people, processes, systems, facilities, controls, and evidence used to manage keys and associated keying material. Its boundary includes governance and operational dependencies that can materially affect lifecycle decisions, authorization, protection, recovery, monitoring, or assurance.

**3.5** **Critical keying material** means keying material classified as Critical under Section 7. The classification applies to the complete keying-material set and relevant authority relationships, not only to an individual secret component. In this Policy, “Critical material,” “Critical key,” and the adjectival form “Critical-key” refer to Critical keying material unless a narrower meaning is expressly stated.

**3.6** **Cryptographic Baseline** means the approved Standard that defines permitted algorithms, parameters, operations, security strength, cryptoperiods, transition status, and implementation restrictions. It provides the decision criteria against which cryptographic selections, continued use, exceptions, and migration needs shall be assessed.

**3.7** **Key Owner** means the accountable business or security function that authorizes a keying-material set's purpose, classification, lifecycle decisions, recovery, and disposition. The Key Owner may delegate performance of approved activities but retains accountability for decisions, risk treatment, required evidence, and oversight.

**3.8** **Keying material** means a cryptographic key and its associated keying material. The term applies to individual components and to a related set when their combination, derivation relationship, or shared authority is relevant to control design or risk.

**3.9** **Protection boundary** means the approved logical, physical, or cryptographic environment in which keying material is generated, stored, processed, recovered, or used. The boundary is defined by enforceable controls, trust assumptions, interfaces, authorized transfer paths, and conditions under which material may enter or leave it.

**3.10** **Security Approval Authority** means the formally appointed, independent role or body authorized to approve Critical-key requirements, material exceptions, and material changes under this Policy. Its independence requires sufficient organizational authority and separation from sole responsibility for implementing or operating the matter being approved.

**3.11** **Signing authority** means a key, signing service, quorum, or other cryptographic authority able to authorize a signature or protocol action. The term encompasses the aggregate capability created by components, approval mechanisms, and recovery or administrative paths that can cause or enable signing.

**3.12** **Transaction Initiator**, **Transaction Approver**, and **Signer** mean the separately attributable roles that create a request, authorize or reject it, and produce a signature only after required controls succeed. Separation applies to the identities and control paths used for the same action so that one role cannot silently perform or bypass another role's decision.

**3.13** **Wallet** means a logical arrangement of keys, addresses, derivation data, authorities, and controls used to manage blockchain assets or authority. A Wallet may span multiple systems, custodians, networks, or signing mechanisms and shall be governed according to the aggregate authority that the arrangement enables.

**3.14** **Web3 keying material** includes blockchain account keys, validator signing keys, withdrawal credentials, treasury keys, contract deployer keys, smart-contract upgrade or governance authority, and related recovery material. It also includes associated material and control dependencies that can create, transfer, restore, or exercise the corresponding on-chain or protocol authority.

**3.15** **Handling attribute** means a controlled designation, independent of impact classification, that defines permitted disclosure or handling conditions; public verification material is one such attribute. **Material change** means a change that could alter classification, aggregate authority, protection, authorization, recoverability, cryptographic suitability, service continuity, compliance, or residual risk sufficiently to require approval or reassessment. The applicable Standard shall define measurable decision criteria and any additional terms needed for consistent implementation.

## 4. Governance and Control Architecture

**4.1** The Company shall operate a documented CKMS proportionate to its services, risk, and dependencies. The CKMS design shall define its scope, governance bodies, control objectives, trust boundaries, and interfaces with business, technology, security, and third-party functions. Its adequacy shall be reviewed after material changes and at a frequency commensurate with the highest-impact keying material it governs.

**4.2** The CKMS shall include this Policy, key-management Processes, technical Standards, Procedures and SOPs, an authoritative inventory, exception management, audit and incident-response arrangements, and a CKMS design specification. These components shall collectively cover the full lifecycle of in-scope keying material and identify how required control outcomes are achieved and evidenced. Missing or conflicting components shall be recorded as governance gaps, assigned to an accountable owner, and remediated or addressed through an approved exception.

**4.3** This Policy is authoritative. Subordinate documents may impose stricter requirements but shall not weaken this Policy. Conflicts shall be escalated to the Policy owner for resolution. Pending resolution, the Company shall preserve all applicable control objectives and use a documented risk decision when confidentiality, integrity, availability, recoverability, or other objectives cannot all be satisfied by the same treatment.

**4.4** The Company shall maintain the following document hierarchy: Each document shall identify its owner, approval authority, effective date, review cycle, and relationship to superior and implementing documents. Document governance shall ensure that changes at one level are assessed for required changes at other levels.

- **4.4.1** **Policy** defines mandatory organizational requirements, governance, and accountability. Policy requirements shall state required outcomes and decision authority without prescribing routine implementation steps.
- **4.4.2** **Process** defines end-to-end flow, state transitions, and control gates. Each Process shall identify accountable roles, entry and exit criteria, exception paths, and the records needed to demonstrate that control gates operated.
- **4.4.3** **Standard** defines mandatory technical, cryptographic, security, and application-specific requirements. Standards shall establish measurable criteria and approved constraints that implementing systems and Procedures must satisfy.
- **4.4.4** **Procedure / SOP** defines approved human or automated actions and required evidence. Procedures and SOPs shall remain consistent with governing Processes and Standards and shall be controlled so that only approved versions are used.

**4.5** For each material Policy requirement, the Company shall maintain traceability to its implementing document, accountable owner, evidence, control source or rationale, known gaps, and approved exceptions. Traceability shall be sufficiently current and specific to support management oversight, independent review, and remediation tracking. Where one implementation supports multiple requirements, the mapping shall distinguish the evidence and control outcome applicable to each requirement.

**4.6** Executive Management shall approve this Policy, its material revisions, and the resources required for implementation. The Security Approval Authority shall own the Policy, recommend material Policy revisions, and approve material operational CKMS changes within formally delegated authority before production use. Approval records shall identify the decision, scope, conditions, effective date, and accountable parties and shall be retained as CKMS governance evidence.

**4.7** Material changes include changes to Critical keying material, a key-management service, authority model, protection boundary, Cryptographic Baseline, recovery design, custody arrangement, or exception. Before approval, the proposed change shall be assessed against the criteria in Clause 3.15 for security, operational, legal, dependency, recovery, and transition impact. The assessment shall define acceptance criteria, required independent review, and any conditions that must be satisfied before production use.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

**5.1** The Company shall assign clear ownership and accountability for each key-management activity. Critical-key activities shall use separation of duties, dual control, or multi-party authorization proportionate to risk. Role assignments and control combinations shall be documented, periodically reviewed, and designed to prevent one party from unilaterally creating, activating, using, recovering, or disposing of Critical authority.

**5.2** Executive Management shall provide governance and accept residual risk only when it exceeds delegated authority. Its decisions shall consider business need, affected assets and authorities, duration, compensating controls, and remediation commitments. Acceptance shall be documented, time-bound where appropriate, and subject to review when the underlying conditions change.

**5.3** The Security Approval Authority shall maintain Policy governance, approve Critical-key requirements and exceptions, oversee material risk assessments and compromise response, and ensure independent review of findings. It shall have sufficient independence, expertise, and organizational authority to challenge designs and withhold approval. Decisions, conditions, dissent, and required remediation shall be recorded and tracked to closure.

**5.4** Each keying-material set shall have a Key Owner. Ownership shall be assigned before production activation and shall remain attributable throughout retention and disposition. A change or vacancy in ownership shall trigger a documented transfer or escalation so that lifecycle decisions are not left unaccountable.

**5.5** The Key Owner shall authorize its purpose, classification, use, lifecycle decisions, recovery, and disposition. The Key Owner shall identify dependencies and participate in access reviews and compromise response. The owner shall confirm that continued use remains justified, required controls and evidence are maintained, and material risks or gaps are escalated through the appropriate governance path.

**5.6** The Security Architecture or designated Cryptographic Authority shall own the Cryptographic Baseline. It shall define requirements for key generation, derivation, import, protection, recovery, transition, use, and destruction. It shall review proposed deviations and technology or protocol constraints, maintain decision rationale, and coordinate changes with affected Key Owners and implementing functions.

**5.7** Platform, key-management-service, node-operations, and application owners shall implement approved controls. They shall maintain attributable access and evidence and support lifecycle, audit, recovery, and incident activities. They shall identify implementation gaps and material changes, prevent production use when mandatory approval criteria are unmet, and maintain evidence sufficient to verify control operation.

**5.8** Key Custodians and Administrators shall perform only approved, attributable actions. They shall not approve their own Critical-key generation, recovery, access elevation, rotation, destruction, or use. Their privileges shall be scoped to assigned duties, reviewed independently, and suspended or removed when no longer required.

**5.9** For a Critical transaction or protocol-control action, the initiator, approver, signer, and Critical-key administrator shall be separated by independently controlled identities and approval paths. The control design shall prevent a single compromised identity or administrative path from satisfying multiple required roles. Evidence shall link each role's attributable decision or action to the same authorized signing context.

**5.10** One natural person shall not hold more than one of these roles for the same action. Temporary delegation or emergency authority shall not circumvent this prohibition and shall follow the controls and review requirements of Section 16. Role records shall permit independent verification of compliance for each action.

**5.11** Critical multi-party approval shall not rely solely on identities, devices, providers, administrative planes, or break-glass paths controlled by one person or one trust domain. Independence shall be assessed across technical control, organizational authority, custody, recovery, and failure modes. Concentration that could defeat the intended quorum shall be eliminated or explicitly treated through approved risk governance.

**5.12** The applicable Standard shall define and periodically test the required independence. Test criteria shall address whether required parties can be bypassed, impersonated, coerced through shared administration, or replaced through a common recovery path. Results, deficiencies, and corrective actions shall be retained as control evidence.

**5.13** Incident Response shall coordinate compromise response. Internal Audit or an independent reviewer shall assess compliance and remediation. These functions shall have timely access to relevant inventory, authorization, lifecycle, and logging evidence while preserving investigative integrity and appropriate confidentiality.

**5.14** Third parties supporting Company keying material shall undergo due diligence. The assessment shall consider the service's authority, protection and recovery model, personnel and administrative access, dependencies, assurance evidence, incident history, concentration risk, and exit feasibility. Approval shall be completed before the third party receives or can exercise material authority and shall be reassessed based on risk and material change.

**5.15** Contracts shall require security obligations, attributable access, material-change and incident notification, assurance, recovery support, data return or destruction, and exit planning. Contractual requirements shall allocate ownership and decision rights for key lifecycle events, evidence access, subservice dependencies, and compromise response. Material deficiencies shall be remediated, subject to an approved exception, or result in restriction or termination of the arrangement.

## 6. Key Inventory

**6.1** The Company shall maintain an accurate, complete, authoritative inventory of in-scope keying material. An inventory record shall be created before production activation or, for discovered assets, as soon as practicable after discovery. The inventory shall support ownership, authorization, lifecycle control, dependency analysis, incident response, recovery, and audit, with material omissions treated as control deficiencies.

**6.2** The inventory shall not contain plaintext secret material. It shall be integrity-protected and accessible to authorized personnel during incidents and recovery. Access, modification, and deletion rights shall be limited and attributable, and changes to material inventory fields shall produce evidence sufficient to establish who changed what and under which authority.

**6.3** Each record shall include, at minimum, the following information. The applicable Standard shall define controlled field formats, required values, validation criteria, and any additional attributes needed for particular key or authority types. A record shall not be considered complete when required data is unknown, internally inconsistent, or unsupported by an approved exception:

- **6.3.1** A unique inventory identifier, owner, classification, handling attribute, and authority type. These attributes shall be assigned from controlled values and shall identify the accountable Key Owner and the governance treatment applicable to the material.
- **6.3.2** Purpose, authorized operations, environment, lifecycle state, and dependency relationships. The record shall distinguish permitted use from technical capability and identify systems, services, authorities, or business functions that depend on continued availability or integrity.
- **6.3.3** Algorithm, key or domain parameters, security strength, cryptoperiod, and activation, expiry, rotation, and disposition dates. Values shall be consistent with the Cryptographic Baseline or reference an approved exception, and dates shall support timely lifecycle decisions before continued use becomes noncompliant.
- **6.3.4** Generation, import, derivation, and parent-child provenance, without exposing a secret. Provenance shall be sufficient to establish the approved origin, method, protection boundary, and relationship of the material while excluding entropy, seeds, private values, reconstructable data, or equivalent secrets.
- **6.3.5** Protection boundary, protection or wrapping method, access roles, custody or quorum design, and recovery arrangement. The record shall identify the approved control model and material dependencies without recording secret credentials or information that would enable unauthorized reconstruction or bypass.
- **6.3.6** Approvals, exceptions, compromise or revocation history, evidence references, and required retention. References shall be durable and sufficient for an authorized reviewer to locate the governing decision and verify lifecycle and remediation outcomes.
- **6.3.7** For Web3 material, the network, protocol role, public identifier, Wallet or authority relationship, and relevant signing or recovery dependency. The record shall distinguish networks and signing domains that could otherwise be confused and shall show how the material contributes to aggregate asset, validator, governance, or recovery authority.

**6.4** The inventory shall represent aggregate authority. It shall identify relationships among seeds, HD roots, derived keys, addresses, controlled assets, validator identities, contract roles, recovery arrangements, and signing authorities. Relationship data shall enable the Company to determine the full impact of compromise, loss, recovery, rotation, or disposition of any related material.

**6.5** The Key Owner shall keep inventory information current. The Company shall reconcile inventory records against deployed services, public identifiers, and provider records at a risk-based frequency defined in the applicable Standard. Reconciliation shall identify unauthorized, missing, stale, duplicated, or inconsistent records, and discrepancies shall be assigned, risk-assessed, and tracked to resolution with evidence retained.

**6.6** Unowned, undocumented, or unclassifiable keying material shall be restricted, assessed, and either brought into compliance or retired. Until disposition is approved, it shall not be granted new authority or used for new production activity except where documented incident, recovery, or continuity needs require controlled use. The assessment and resulting ownership, classification, remediation, or retirement decision shall be recorded.

## 7. Key Classification and Handling

**7.1** The Company shall assign an impact classification to each keying-material set. Classification shall be completed before production activation, recorded in the authoritative inventory, and approved by the Key Owner. Material whose impact cannot yet be determined shall receive the more restrictive plausible classification until the assessment is completed.

**7.2** Classification shall consider compromise, misuse, modification, unavailability, loss of recoverability, asset exposure, protocol control, validator risk, dependency breadth, and legal or contractual impact. The assessment shall consider both the material itself and the aggregate authority created by related keys, shares, credentials, recovery paths, systems, and delegated roles. The rationale and relevant impact scenarios shall be documented as evidence and reviewed after material changes.

**7.3** The Company shall use the following impact classifications. The applicable Standard shall define measurable classification criteria and examples consistent with these treatments. When multiple criteria apply, the highest resulting impact classification shall govern:

| Classification | Policy treatment |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | Compromise, misuse, or loss could materially affect assets, protocol control, consensus participation, recovery, service continuity, or organizational trust. |
| **High** | Material protects sensitive services, privileged authentication, high-impact signing, or sensitive data. |
| **Standard** | Material supports ordinary internal authentication, encryption, or integrity services. |

**7.4** Public verification material is a handling attribute, not an impact classification. The public attribute permits authorized disclosure but does not remove integrity, availability, provenance, inventory, or lifecycle requirements. Handling attributes shall be recorded separately from impact classification so that disclosure permissions do not weaken other controls.

**7.5** A public key, address, certificate, trust anchor, or verification record may still be Critical or High when its integrity, binding, or authority relationship is material. Classification shall reflect the consequence of substitution, misbinding, deletion, stale publication, or unauthorized change even when confidentiality is not required. Publication and change controls shall preserve an attributable, verifiable relationship between the public material and its approved owner or authority.

**7.6** Classification shall determine protection, authorization, separation of duties, monitoring, recovery, review cadence, cryptoperiod, and audit depth. Processes and Standards shall translate each classification into minimum control outcomes and may impose stricter controls based on authority type, environment, or threat exposure. A lower classification or reduced control set requires documented reassessment and approval by the Key Owner, with independent approval where the material was Critical.

**7.7** Every Critical keying-material set shall have named ownership, individually attributable access, independent approval for material lifecycle changes, protected audit logging, and documented recovery. These controls shall be established and evidenced before activation, and any control that becomes ineffective shall trigger restriction, risk assessment, and remediation. Evidence shall identify the accountable roles, approval decision, effective control configuration, and applicable inventory record.

**7.8** It shall also have periodic access review and documented compromise response. Review frequency and response readiness shall reflect the authority, exposure, and recovery complexity of the material and shall meet the minimum cadence in the applicable Standard. Review and exercise results, deficiencies, owners, and target remediation dates shall be retained.

**7.9** The Company shall distinguish impact classification from authority type. Authority types include validator, asset-control, protocol-control, application-security, infrastructure-security, and recovery authority. Each inventory record shall identify both dimensions, and controls shall address the combined risks rather than treating either dimension as sufficient by itself. A keying-material set with multiple authority types shall be governed according to its full aggregate authority.

## 8. Key Lifecycle Management

**8.1** The Company shall manage each keying-material set throughout its lifecycle. An approved Process shall define lifecycle states, permitted transitions, accountable roles, control gates, and required evidence from planning through final disposition. The authoritative inventory shall reflect the current state and material dependencies.

**8.2** The lifecycle includes identification, planning, classification, cryptographic selection, generation, derivation, import or receipt, registration, provisioning, activation, and authorized use. Each stage shall have entry and completion criteria proportionate to classification and authority type, and no stage shall bypass required approval or protection controls. Records shall link the material to its origin, owner, intended use, and approved protection boundary.

**8.3** It also includes backup and recovery, rotation and migration, suspension, deactivation, revocation, archival, and destruction. The applicable Process shall distinguish reversible restrictions from permanent disposition and define the authority required for each transition. Lifecycle planning shall account for backups, replicas, derived material, provider-held copies, relying parties, and recovery paths.

**8.4** Before production activation, the Company shall approve the owner, purpose, classification, authorized operations, cryptographic selection, protection boundary, access model, dependencies, and recovery method. Approval shall verify that required controls are implemented, tested where appropriate, and represented in the inventory. Critical material requires Key Owner approval and independent approval from the designated authority for the material lifecycle decision.

**8.5** It shall also approve the lifecycle and transition plan, cryptoperiod, and disposition requirements. The plan shall define expected rotation or migration triggers, continuity needs, predecessor and successor handling, retention, and evidence of final disposition. Unresolved dependencies or exceptions shall be explicitly recorded, assigned an owner, and accepted under Section 16 before activation.

**8.6** Secret material shall be generated or derived only by an approved method within an approved protection boundary. The generation event shall be attributable, protected from unauthorized observation or interference, and recorded without exposing the secret. Critical generation shall follow an approved ceremony or automated control flow that enforces the required authorization and separation of duties.

**8.7** The method shall use an approved random bit generator (RBG) with adequate security strength and shall protect entropy inputs and derivation material. Where the term cryptographically secure pseudorandom number generator (CSPRNG) is used in an implementation, the applicable Standard shall establish that it satisfies the approved RBG requirements for that use. The Standard shall define assurance, health-check, seeding, reseeding, and failure-handling outcomes, and a detected or suspected randomness failure shall prevent activation and trigger assessment of all potentially affected material.

**8.8** Imported keying material shall have documented provenance, integrity, cryptographic suitability, and transfer protections before production activation. The receiving function shall verify authorization, origin, expected identity or fingerprint, permitted use, and that no uncontrolled copy or exposure was introduced during transfer. Unverifiable or nonconforming imports shall be quarantined from production use until remediated or approved under Section 16.

**8.9** HD roots, mnemonics, seeds, and derived keys shall follow an approved derivation and generation Standard. The Standard shall address derivation domains and paths, parent-child relationships, collision or reuse avoidance, authorized discoverability, recovery, and the aggregate authority of the root. Inventory and evidence shall permit dependencies and affected descendants to be identified without recording plaintext secret material.

**8.10** Lifecycle changes shall be authorized, recorded in the inventory, supported by proportionate evidence, and preserve required continuity, historical verification, decryption, recovery, and retention capabilities. Evidence shall identify the requested change, approvers, actors, date, prior and resulting states, validation outcome, and affected dependencies. Failed or partially completed transitions shall be contained, reconciled, and escalated according to their risk.

**8.11** No key shall be used outside its authorized purpose, environment, lifecycle state, operation, or cryptoperiod. Technical and procedural controls shall prevent or detect prohibited use and shall fail closed where unauthorized use could create material impact. Any detected use outside these constraints shall be treated as a control failure and assessed for compromise.

**8.12** A deactivated key may be reactivated for operational use only when it is not compromised, its cryptoperiod and any applicable originator-usage period remain valid, and documented reassessment confirms continuing cryptographic suitability, integrity, ownership, access, protection, dependencies, and recovery arrangements. Archived keying material recovered after its applicable usage period may be used only to process information previously protected with that material, including authorized decryption or signature verification; it shall not apply new cryptographic protection or exercise new authority. Approval and the resulting state change shall be recorded, with independent approval required for Critical keying material.

**8.13** Confirmed compromised secret material shall not be returned to active use. It may be retained only for an approved, controlled historical-verification, decryption, evidence, or transition purpose. Retention shall use a restricted protection boundary, prohibit new authority or protection operations, define access and monitoring, and establish an expiry or disposition event. The inventory and incident record shall identify the compromise status, retained purpose, approving authority, and eventual destruction or archival outcome.

## 9. Key Protection and Cryptographic Selection

**9.1** The Company shall protect secret keying material against unauthorized disclosure, modification, substitution, destruction, and use throughout generation, storage, processing, backup, recovery, transfer, and retirement. Protection measures shall be proportionate to classification, authority type, exposure, and aggregate authority and shall remain effective across operational, emergency, and maintenance conditions. Control design and evidence shall address every location and party able to access or reconstruct the material.

**9.2** Secret material shall remain within an approved protection boundary. The boundary shall be documented, inventoried, and subject to access, configuration, monitoring, continuity, and change controls appropriate to the material it protects. Movement across or change to the boundary requires prior authorization, protected transfer, and verification of the receiving boundary.

**9.3** It shall be subject to least privilege, attributable identity, strong authentication for privileged use, access review, and protection against unauthorized export, copying, and persistence. Access shall be limited by approved role and operation, promptly revoked when no longer required, and monitored for anomalous or failed activity. Shared or emergency access shall preserve individual accountability and comply with approved exception or emergency-authority requirements.

**9.4** Secret material shall not be stored in source code, container images, build artifacts, tickets, chat systems, ordinary collaboration storage, logs, or other unapproved locations. Preventive and detective controls shall address accidental inclusion, synchronization, caching, diagnostic output, and residual copies. Discovery in an unapproved location shall trigger immediate restriction, secure removal where evidence permits, exposure assessment, and compromise response when warranted.

**9.5** Critical secret material, including Critical seeds, HD roots, private keys, and reconstructable shares, shall be non-exportable by default. It shall be protected by approved hardware, threshold, or equivalent mechanisms. The selected mechanism shall enforce the required access, authorization, separation, reconstruction, backup, monitoring, and lifecycle outcomes rather than relying on product designation alone. Export capability, if technically present, shall be disabled or independently controlled and monitored.

**9.6** An exception involving plaintext Critical secret material shall be time-bound and approved under Section 16. The request shall demonstrate why no compliant alternative meets the legitimate need and shall identify the exact material, participants, location, duration, exposure path, and compensating controls. Approval shall occur before handling unless a predefined emergency authority expressly permits the action.

**9.7** The exception shall require dual control, protected transport, ephemeral handling, attributable evidence, and post-handling rotation or equivalent risk treatment. The applicable Procedure or SOP shall define participant instructions, start and completion steps, interruption handling, copy control, and secure cleanup. Evidence shall confirm the approved scope, actions performed, validation result, residual copies, and completion of required follow-up treatment without recording the secret.

**9.8** The Company shall protect public keys, certificates, addresses, trust anchors, and verification metadata from unauthorized modification, substitution, misbinding, and untrusted publication. Creation, approval, publication, replacement, and withdrawal shall use attributable sources and integrity-verifiable channels appropriate to the relying-party risk. Material changes shall be validated against the intended owner, authority, network, or service and retained as auditable evidence.

**9.9** The Company shall use separate keys for separate security purposes unless an approved cryptographic design permits otherwise. Separation shall account for operation, environment, application, tenant, authority type, and conflicting lifecycle or recovery requirements. Any approved multi-purpose use shall document compatibility, aggregate impact, access implications, cryptoperiod, compromise scope, and migration treatment.

**9.10** The Cryptographic Baseline shall define the approved algorithm, parameters, operation, security strength, cryptoperiod, status, permitted use, owner, and migration treatment for each cryptographic profile. Selection shall consider the material's purpose, classification, protocol and interoperability constraints, expected protection period, implementation assurance, and transition needs. Baseline decisions, approvals, review dates, and supporting rationale shall be retained.

**9.11** The Baseline shall distinguish acceptable, deprecated, legacy-use, and disallowed mechanisms. Each status shall have unambiguous permitted operations, implementation constraints, approval requirements, and transition triggers. Systems and inventory records shall support identification of deployed mechanisms by status and owner.

**9.12** Legacy-use mechanisms shall not create new cryptographic protection, signatures, encryption, or key wrapping. Their use shall be limited to specifically approved processing of existing protected information or verification of historical artifacts when continued access is required. Access shall be constrained, monitored, and removed when the approved dependency or retention need ends.

**9.13** Deprecated mechanisms shall have a time-bound risk acceptance and transition plan. Disallowed mechanisms shall not be used. The transition plan shall identify affected material and dependencies, accountable owners, compensating controls, milestones, validation, and completion date. Missed milestones or newly discovered use shall be escalated and re-evaluated under Section 16 rather than treated as implicit approval.

**9.14** The Cryptographic Baseline shall document externally mandated algorithms or formats as interoperability constraints, together with their risk, compensating controls, permitted uses, and feasible migration or exit treatment. The assessment shall identify the dependency, authority or information exposed, limitations on cryptoperiod or rotation, and conditions requiring suspension, migration, or acceptance by the appropriate authority. The Company shall monitor relevant external changes and review the treatment when assumptions or available alternatives change.

**9.15** The use of an HSM, MPC, multisig, hardware wallet, cold storage, remote signer, or similar technology shall not by itself satisfy authorization or governance requirements. Each implementation shall be assessed against required outcomes for ownership, independent approval, access control, transaction authorization, protection, recovery, monitoring, compromise response, lifecycle management, and exit. Material design choices and residual risks shall receive approval appropriate to classification and be supported by evidence of implementation and testing.

## 10. Key Access, Usage, and Transaction Authorization

**10.1** The Company shall authorize key access and use by purpose, classification, authority type, role, environment, lifecycle state, and approved operation. Each authorization shall identify the accountable approver, the subject or workload receiving authority, its permitted scope, and its effective and expiry conditions. Authorization shall be denied when the requested use cannot be matched to a current inventory record and approved business purpose.

**10.2** Access shall be individually or workload-attributable, least privileged, periodically reviewed, logged, and promptly removed when no longer required. Shared identities shall not be used where they prevent attribution, and workload identities shall be uniquely bound to an approved service and environment. Reviews shall reconcile granted access with current role, purpose, and key inventory records and shall document revocation or remediation of discrepancies.

**10.3** Key use shall be limited to approved cryptographic operations. A key shall not be repurposed for unrelated signing, encryption, authentication, derivation, recovery, or protocol control without documented risk assessment and approval. The assessment shall address separation of purpose, aggregate authority, cryptographic suitability, dependency impact, and whether a distinct key is required before the new use begins.

**10.4** Critical transactions and protocol-control actions shall require an approved authorization decision before signing. The decision shall be made through an approved path by the required independently attributable approvers and shall identify the governing policy rules and approval threshold. Authorization evidence shall be retained in a form that permits the Company to establish who approved what action, when, and under which authority.

**10.5** A signer shall fail closed and shall not generate a signature when required authorization or transaction-policy controls do not succeed. Missing, expired, malformed, conflicting, or unverifiable authorization shall be treated as a failed control rather than as permission to proceed. Failure handling shall prevent automatic bypass, alert the responsible function according to risk, and preserve evidence sufficient to diagnose the decision.

**10.6** The authorization decision shall be bound to the exact final signing payload or digest. The binding shall be integrity-protected and verifiable at the signing boundary so that a change after approval invalidates the authorization. Records shall permit later confirmation that the payload presented to the signer was the payload authorized, without requiring retention of secret material.

**10.7** It shall be bound to the applicable network or chain identifier, signing domain, source authority, sender, nonce or equivalent replay control, recipient or counterparty, asset, value, contract and function restrictions, and expiry. Each applicable element shall be validated against the approved request and authoritative context before signing, and omitted elements shall be justified by the transaction type. A mismatch, ambiguity, stale value, or unsupported context shall cause rejection and escalation according to classification.

**10.8** The applicable Standard shall define fee limits, frequency or velocity limits, transaction simulation or semantic validation where feasible, typed-data validation, and signer-side controls. It shall specify when each control is mandatory, the sources of trusted context, rejection behavior, and the authority permitted to change thresholds. Material control changes shall be approved, tested, recorded, and implemented without creating an unreviewed path around existing authorization.

**10.9** Blind or opaque signing of Critical transactions is prohibited unless a documented exception defines compensating controls. The exception shall identify why the signing intent cannot be independently rendered or validated, the bounded transactions and duration, additional approval and verification steps, monitoring, and termination conditions. Signing shall not proceed when the compensating controls cannot provide approvers with sufficient trustworthy context to understand the material effect of the action.

**10.10** For each Critical signing authority, the Company shall define approval thresholds, permitted network, source authority, permitted destination, permitted asset, value limits, and contract and function restrictions. These constraints shall be approved by the Key Owner and independently reviewed before activation or material change. Enforcement shall deny by default any action outside the defined constraints, and evidence shall identify the applicable rule set and decision outcome.

**10.11** It shall also define emergency authority and monitoring requirements. Emergency authority shall be limited to predefined triggers, identities, actions, values, destinations, and duration and shall remain subject to the requirements of Section 16. Monitoring shall provide timely detection of attempted and completed use, authorization failures, policy overrides, and anomalous activity and shall route alerts to a function capable of acting independently of the initiator.

**10.12** Remote signers shall use mutually authenticated, attributable workloads, network isolation, per-key authorization, and signer-side validation. A remote signer shall not rely solely on an upstream client for Critical policy enforcement. The Company shall restrict administrative paths, protect credentials and policy configuration, detect replay or unauthorized routing, and retain correlated request, authorization, and signing records. Loss of authentication, policy state, required telemetry, or trusted network context shall cause the signer to reject Critical signing requests.

## 11. Backup and Recovery

**11.1** The Company shall maintain documented, tested, classification-appropriate backup and recovery arrangements. Each arrangement shall identify accountable ownership, covered material and dependencies, authorized recovery conditions, required participants, and restoration priorities. The arrangement shall be reviewed after material changes to the key, protection boundary, authority model, custody model, or dependent service.

**11.2** These arrangements apply to material whose loss would impair required operations, security services, asset control, protocol participation, or required records. The Key Owner shall document whether recovery is required, prohibited, or provided through replacement or authority transfer rather than restoration. Dependencies needed to make recovered material usable and safe, including policy, configuration, metadata, and protocol state, shall be included in recovery planning.

**11.3** Backup and recovery material shall be protected at least as strongly as the authority it enables. It shall not create untracked, persistent, or routine operational copies of secret material. Copies, shares, and recovery credentials shall be inventoried or verifiably referenced, access-controlled, integrity-protected, and subject to lifecycle and disposition requirements. Recovery material shall not be colocated or administered in a manner that defeats the intended separation or quorum.

**11.4** Recovery shall require documented authorization, integrity validation, attributable access, and evidence. Before restored material is activated, responsible personnel shall validate its identity, provenance, lifecycle state, policy configuration, dependencies, and fitness for the intended environment. Evidence shall identify the request, approvals, participants, material recovered, validation results, exposure created, and final disposition of temporary artifacts.

**11.5** For Critical material, recovery shall require Key Owner authorization and independent approval, except under the narrowly defined emergency authority in Section 16. Authorization shall be bound to the specific material, recovery objective, participants, destination protection boundary, permitted activation, and time window. No participant able to reconstruct or activate the material shall provide the sole independent approval for that recovery.

**11.6** Critical recovery design shall avoid a single person, identity system, administrative plane, provider, or physical event being able to reconstruct the material or bypass the required approval path. The design shall consider correlated compromise, site loss, provider failure, insider action, and loss of a quorum participant. Any unavoidable concentration shall be documented as risk, protected by compensating controls, and approved through the applicable exception process.

**11.7** Recovery testing shall occur at a risk-based frequency defined in the applicable Standard. Tests shall demonstrate safe recovery without unnecessary exposure and shall produce evidence of the result, gaps, and remediation. Test scope shall include authorization, quorum operation, integrity checks, dependency restoration, activation controls, and cleanup or destruction of test artifacts. A combined compromise-and-recovery exercise may satisfy this clause and Clause 12.9 when it covers and evidences every objective required by both clauses. A test is complete only when outcomes are reconciled to objectives, deficiencies have assigned owners and due dates, and the inventory and recovery documentation reflect material findings.

**11.8** The Backup and Recovery Standard shall define recovery objectives, quorum, geographic or custody separation, backup integrity, emergency recovery, physical handling, transportation, and implementation methods. It shall also define test scenarios, activation and rollback criteria, evidence and retention requirements, temporary-material handling, and post-recovery reconciliation. Requirements shall distinguish recovery of cryptographic material from restoration of authority, service, or protocol state where those outcomes require separate controls.

## 12. Key Compromise Management

**12.1** Suspected or confirmed compromise of keying material shall be treated as a security incident. Personnel and systems shall report indicators promptly through approved channels without attempting unapproved validation or remediation that could increase exposure or destroy evidence. Incident severity, response authority, and escalation shall reflect the material's classification, aggregate authority, and affected dependencies.

**12.2** Compromise includes unauthorized disclosure, acquisition, access, copying, export, derivation, use, modification, loss, destruction, loss of control, or use of an unapproved protection boundary or signing path. It also includes credible uncertainty about custody, integrity, authorization, exclusivity, or the ability to prevent further use. Absence of observed misuse shall not by itself establish that keying material remains trustworthy.

**12.3** For Critical keying material, a suspected compromise shall trigger immediate restriction or suspension of use. Continued use is permitted only when suspension would create greater and imminent risk and the predefined emergency authority in Clauses 16.8 through 16.12 is invoked with its required approvals, bounded scope, automatic expiry, recording, monitoring, and independent review. Continued use shall not be treated as a determination that the material is uncompromised.

**12.4** The Company shall detect, contain, assess, and record the event. It shall preserve evidence and identify dependencies and related keying material. Assessment shall address the earliest plausible exposure, accessible authority, affected copies or shares, transactions or operations performed, protection and authorization paths, and potential persistence. Containment actions and evidence handling shall be time-stamped, attributable, and coordinated to avoid preventable loss of forensic or operational information.

**12.5** It shall determine required replacement, rotation, migration, revocation, or notification and complete post-incident remediation. Decisions shall consider whether trust can be re-established, whether related or derived material is affected, and whether on-chain, relying-party, provider, customer, legal, or contractual action is required. Remediation is complete only when successor authority and required services are validated, predecessor paths are disabled or controlled, affected state is reconciled, residual risk is approved, and assigned corrective actions are tracked to closure.

**12.6** The Critical-key compromise process shall identify notification recipients and content, assigned responders, and the re-key or authority-transfer method. It shall define decision authority, communication security, sequencing, fallback and rollback conditions, external coordination, and evidence ownership before an incident occurs. Notifications shall be timely and accurate, avoid disclosure of secret material, and be updated when material facts or required protective actions change.

**12.7** It shall identify affected signatures, transactions, data, and protocol authorities, completion criteria, and post-incident monitoring. The Company shall determine the validity and business or security effect of actions taken during the plausible compromise window and document any action that cannot be conclusively resolved. Completion criteria shall be measurable and shall include reconciliation of inventories, access, assets, authorities, dependencies, notifications, and retained evidence.

**12.8** Compromise response for Critical material shall prioritize prevention of unauthorized signing, asset loss, protocol-control loss, validator penalties, continued compromise, and loss of forensic evidence. Response leaders shall document material tradeoffs when these priorities conflict, including the basis, accountable decision maker, safeguards, and required follow-up. Actions shall minimize further use or exposure of suspected material while preserving the ability to transfer authority and maintain essential safety.

**12.9** The Company shall exercise Critical compromise and recovery plans at the frequency defined by the applicable Standard and retain after-action evidence. Exercises shall test decision authority, communications, containment, signer restriction, replacement or authority transfer, dependency reconciliation, and restoration under realistic failure conditions. A combined exercise may also satisfy Clause 11.7 when it covers and evidences every required recovery-testing objective. Evidence shall record objectives, participants, decisions, timing, outcomes, gaps, and assigned remediation, and the exercise shall not be closed until material findings have owners and due dates.

## 13. Key Rotation and Migration

**13.1** The Company shall rotate, renew, replace, or migrate keying material before continued use no longer meets approved cryptographic, operational, or risk requirements. The Key Owner shall ensure that the need is assessed early enough to complete authorization, testing, dependency updates, and cutover without an avoidable lapse in protection or service. Completion shall be supported by evidence that the successor material is operational and the predecessor has reached its approved disposition.

**13.2** Rotation or migration shall be triggered by cryptoperiod expiry, cryptographic transition, suspected compromise, or material change in authority or exposure. Suspected compromise shall be handled under Section 12, with rotation or migration sequenced according to containment priorities rather than routine scheduling. Trigger decisions, urgency, responsible parties, and any interim safeguards shall be recorded and monitored through completion.

**13.3** It shall also be triggered by a protection-boundary or provider change, recovery-design inadequacy, certificate or trust transition, or protocol lifecycle requirement. The Key Owner shall assess whether the event affects related keys, trust relationships, recovery material, or aggregate authority and shall define the resulting migration scope. Deferral shall require documented risk treatment and approval under Section 16 where a Policy requirement cannot be met.

**13.4** The Cryptographic Baseline shall define maximum cryptoperiods and, where applicable, originator- and recipient-usage periods by key type, algorithm, operation, and authority type. Periods shall reflect the material's classification, exposure, use volume, dependencies, and consequences of compromise or unavailability. The Baseline owner shall review these periods when cryptographic guidance, protocol requirements, threat conditions, or operating models materially change.

**13.5** It shall identify the responsible owner, advance notification, and required rotation evidence. Evidence shall demonstrate authorization, successful successor validation, dependency and inventory updates, cutover outcome, and predecessor disposition or approved retention. Missed notifications, overdue rotations, and incomplete evidence shall be escalated according to classification and reported through CKMS compliance monitoring.

**13.6** The Company shall authorize and validate successor keying material and identify dependencies and transition requirements. Validation shall confirm that the successor has the approved purpose, classification, cryptographic profile, protection boundary, access model, recovery arrangement, and authority before activation. The transition decision and validation results shall be attributable, retained, and independently approved where required for Critical material.

**13.7** It shall update access, inventory, monitoring, and recovery arrangements. It shall retire predecessor material only after required continuity and retention obligations are resolved. The Company shall verify that dependent systems and authorized parties use the successor, monitor for unexpected predecessor use, and record any residual dependency or retained historical purpose.

**13.8** Applicable Processes and Standards shall define sequencing, compatibility handling, validator migration, authority transfer, asset migration, client checks, rollback, and evidence requirements. The sequence shall prevent simultaneous uncontrolled authority, gaps in required service, loss of recovery, and unsafe signing or transaction states. Material deviations, rollback events, and unresolved validation failures shall be logged, risk-assessed, and reviewed before the migration is closed.

## 14. Key Revocation, Archival, and Destruction

**14.1** The Company shall suspend, deactivate, revoke, archive, or destroy keying material when purpose ends, authorization is withdrawn, or compromise occurs. The Key Owner shall select and authorize the disposition according to classification, authority type, dependency, recovery, and retention needs, with compromise-driven actions coordinated under Section 12. The action, effective time, responsible roles, and outcome shall be recorded in the inventory and supporting evidence.

**14.2** It shall also do so when a successor is activated, cryptoperiod or retention requirements require transition, or risk otherwise requires disposition. Disposition shall be initiated promptly after the trigger and sequenced to preserve required continuity, verification, decryption, evidence, and legal obligations. Overdue or blocked disposition shall be documented, monitored, and escalated according to the risk of continued authority or retention.

**14.3** For certificates and other relying-party trust material, revocation shall include prompt, attributable notification to affected relying parties or services. The Company shall identify recipients and available trust-distribution channels in advance where practicable and shall track delivery or other confirmation of effective revocation. Failed or delayed notification shall trigger escalation and compensating monitoring proportionate to the affected trust.

**14.4** The notification shall identify the material, effective time, and reason without exposing secret material. It shall provide sufficient non-secret identifiers and required recipient actions to prevent continued reliance. The Company shall retain the approved notice, distribution record, delivery outcome, and any unresolved recipient response as revocation evidence.

**14.5** For a private key whose corresponding account or authority is recognized on-chain, an internal revocation designation or disabled signer shall not be treated as sufficient by itself. Protocol authority remains until the relevant on-chain role, account authority, quorum, or controlled assets are changed or otherwise made unusable. The Company shall therefore treat revocation as a coordinated authority-removal or risk-containment activity, with completion supported by evidence of the resulting on-chain state and any remaining capabilities of the predecessor.

**14.6** The Company shall disable the signing path and create and validate successor authority. These actions shall be sequenced to prevent unauthorized use while avoiding an unapproved loss of required control, recovery, or service continuity. Validation shall be independently reviewed where required and shall confirm that the successor authority and its access, monitoring, and recovery controls operate as approved.

**14.7** It shall remove or replace on-chain roles, quorum members, withdrawal authorities, delegations, and allowlists. It shall reconcile the resulting on-chain state. Reconciliation shall compare the approved target state with authoritative protocol records, identify pending or failed changes, and preserve non-secret evidence of final authority assignments.

**14.8** The Company shall identify required asset movement, authority transfer, validator exit or migration, counterparty notification, monitoring of predecessor authority, and pre-cutover transaction impact before final disposal. The disposition plan shall assign accountable owners, authorization gates, sequence, completion criteria, and treatment of failed or delayed actions. Monitoring shall continue until unauthorized predecessor use and residual exposure are reduced to the approved level.

**14.9** Before disposition, the Company shall assess dependencies, backups, recovery material, certificates, trust relationships, retention needs, historical verification, legal or contractual obligations, and protocol-specific requirements. The assessment shall determine what must be revoked, retained, transferred, archived, or destroyed and shall prevent overlooked copies or dependent services from preserving unintended authority. Findings, approvals, exceptions, and residual obligations shall be linked to the inventory record.

**14.10** Destruction shall use an approved method intended to render secret material and recovery copies infeasible to recover. Destruction shall cover authorized replicas, backups, shares, temporary copies, and provider-held material within the approved scope, unless documented retention is required. The Company shall verify completion to the extent practicable and restrict and monitor any material that cannot yet be destroyed.

**14.11** Evidence shall identify the inventory record, responsible roles, approvals, method, date, verification result, retained metadata, and residual risk. For Critical material, evidence shall also demonstrate required independent authorization and review and account for known copies and recovery paths. Incomplete, contradictory, or failed destruction evidence shall remain an open compliance issue until remediated or accepted under Section 16.

## 15. Logging, Monitoring, Audit, and Compliance

**15.1** The Company shall generate, protect, retain, and review security-relevant key-management records sufficient to establish accountability, detect unauthorized activity, support incident response, and demonstrate compliance. Record scope, review frequency, retention, and escalation shall be proportionate to classification, authority type, exposure, and applicable obligations. Identified gaps shall be tracked to remediation or formally accepted through the applicable governance process.

**15.2** Records shall cover lifecycle events, access and administrative activity, authorization and key use, backup and recovery, configuration changes, compromise response, exceptions, and destruction. Coverage shall include successful, denied, failed, and materially incomplete actions and shall preserve the sequence needed to reconstruct significant events. The applicable Standard shall define required event sources and retention periods without recording plaintext secret material.

**15.3** Records shall identify time, inventory identifier, attributable actor or workload, action, outcome, authorization context, and evidence reference. Where a field cannot be recorded directly, the Company shall maintain a protected, verifiable correlation to the authoritative source. Missing attribution, inconsistent time, or broken evidence references shall be treated as control deficiencies and investigated according to risk.

**15.4** Critical signing records shall identify the approved signing context or a non-secret, verifiable reference to it. The record shall permit an independent reviewer to determine whether the signature matched the authorized payload, network or domain, authority, purpose, approval, and validity period. Mismatches or inability to verify the authorization binding shall be escalated for incident and compliance assessment.

**15.5** Records shall not expose plaintext secret material. Logging and monitoring shall be protected against unauthorized access, alteration, deletion, suppression, and disclosure. Access to sensitive records shall be least privileged and attributable, and retention or disposal shall preserve investigative and legal needs while limiting unnecessary exposure.

**15.6** Critical-key logs shall use protected time synchronization and a tamper-evident or independently retained record. Material logging or monitoring failures shall be detected and escalated according to risk. The Company shall assess affected periods and activities, apply compensating oversight where feasible, preserve evidence of the failure, and verify restoration before closing the issue.

**15.7** The Company shall conduct risk-based audits and independent reviews of inventory, classification, access, lifecycle evidence, recovery readiness, protection, baseline adherence, exceptions, third-party arrangements, and remediation. Reviews shall use defined scope, criteria, sampling or coverage, evidence sources, and accountable reporting, and shall consider both control design and operating effectiveness. Findings shall have severity, ownership, due dates, tracked remediation, and documented validation or risk acceptance before closure.

**15.8** Critical-key domains shall receive heightened independent review. The review frequency and depth shall reflect aggregate authority, asset or protocol impact, prior incidents, control changes, exceptions, third-party dependencies, and unresolved findings. Results shall be reported to the Security Approval Authority and relevant Key Owners, with material deficiencies escalated to Executive Management when delegated risk authority is exceeded.

## 16. Exception Management and Emergency Authority

**16.1** An exception is permitted only when a Policy requirement cannot be met, the need is legitimate, and the resulting risk is explicitly assessed, accepted, and managed. The request shall demonstrate why a compliant alternative is not reasonably available and shall limit the deviation to the minimum scope and duration necessary. Operational convenience, cost avoidance, or an undocumented protocol limitation is not sufficient justification.

**16.2** Each exception shall identify the affected requirement and material; accountable owner; justification; classification and authority type; risk; and compensating controls. The assessment shall address confidentiality, integrity, availability, recoverability, aggregate authority, and any exposure to unauthorized signing, asset movement, protocol control, or validator penalties. References to affected inventory records and implementing documents shall be included without disclosing secret material.

**16.3** It shall also identify monitoring, remediation owner and plan, approvals, review frequency, and expiry date. The remediation plan shall contain measurable completion criteria and, where applicable, milestones for restoring compliance, rotating affected material, or transferring authority. Evidence of each review, control operation, and remediation outcome shall be retained with the exception record.

**16.4** Exceptions affecting Critical material, protocol-control authority, validator authority, recovery material, plaintext secret handling, or deprecated cryptography require Key Owner and Security Approval Authority approval. Approvers shall evaluate both the isolated risk and the aggregate authority created through related keys, shares, identities, providers, and recovery paths. Approval shall occur before the exception is used unless immediate emergency action is expressly permitted under Clauses 16.8 through 16.12.

**16.5** Executive risk acceptance is required when delegated authority is exceeded. The acceptance shall state the residual risk, business necessity, duration, and conditions under which operations must stop or the matter must be escalated. Executive acceptance does not replace required technical, Key Owner, or Security Approval Authority review.

**16.6** An exception shall not authorize unbounded plaintext secret handling, untraceable privileged access, uncontrolled Critical-key copies, or indefinite deprecated cryptography. Any permitted handling shall remain attributable, time-bound, purpose-limited, monitored, and subject to verified disposition. Controls that cannot provide those minimum properties shall result in rejection of the request or suspension of the affected activity.

**16.7** It shall not authorize known unsafe validator operation or bypass of required transaction authorization. In particular, an exception shall not permit conflicting validator signing, activation without verified safety state, or signing a Critical transaction without binding approval to the final signing context. Where safe operation cannot be established, the validator or signing path shall fail closed.

**16.8** Emergency signing or key-management action may occur only under a predefined emergency authority. The authority shall be documented, approved, technically constrained where feasible, and tested before use so that emergency access does not become an alternate routine control path. Its invocation shall be limited to conditions in which delay would create a greater and imminent risk to assets, protocol authority, validator safety, recoverability, or service continuity.

**16.9** It shall define the trigger, authorized identities, permitted actions, transaction, asset, value, destination, duration, automatic expiry, and required audit record. It shall also define required quorum or dual control, prohibited actions, affected networks and signing domains, notification recipients, and revocation or rotation steps after use. Emergency authority shall be no broader than necessary to address the stated trigger.

**16.10** Emergency action shall be recorded contemporaneously. The record shall capture the initiating condition, attributable participants, approvals, exact action and signing context, affected inventory records, timestamps, control overrides, and outcome. If contemporaneous recording would itself impede containment, the minimum available tamper-evident record shall be created and completed as soon as the impediment ends.

**16.11** It shall receive independent review within one business day. The reviewer shall determine whether invocation was authorized and proportionate, whether required controls and notifications operated, and whether any unauthorized access, signing, asset movement, validator exposure, or policy breach occurred. Review outcomes shall document findings, residual risk, corrective actions, owners, deadlines, and any required incident or exception escalation.

**16.12** It shall reconcile affected keys, transactions, assets, and protocol authorities within the period defined by the applicable Standard. Reconciliation shall compare internal approvals and records with signer, provider, network, on-chain, and inventory evidence, including any change to aggregate authority. Unexplained differences shall be treated as a security incident, and closure shall require documented verification that emergency authority has expired or been revoked and that required rotation, recovery, or remediation is complete.

## 17. Web3-Specific Key Management Principles

### 17.0 Application and Source Attribution

**17.0.1** The following controls address Web3 and protocol-specific risk. They are not represented as direct NIST requirements unless separately mapped as such. The Company shall preserve this distinction in control mappings, audits, exceptions, and policy maintenance so that NIST-informed governance is not confused with organization-defined treatment of blockchain, validator, and on-chain authority risks.

### 17.1 Authority Separation

**17.1.1** Validator signing, withdrawal, treasury, transaction, contract deployment, contract upgrade, governance, and recovery authority shall be separately inventoried, classified, and governed according to distinct purpose and impact. Access, approval, monitoring, recovery, and lifecycle controls shall reflect the consequence of each authority rather than treating all blockchain keys as equivalent. Separation shall extend to related service credentials and administrative paths where their compromise could exercise or reconstruct the authority.

**17.1.2** A shared design requires documented risk assessment and approval. Shared cryptographic material shall not silently expand authorization beyond its documented aggregate authority. The assessment shall identify every purpose and authority reachable through the shared design, justify why separation is not feasible, and define compensating authorization, monitoring, recovery, and migration controls.

### 17.2 Aggregate Authority and Wallet Governance

**17.2.1** The Company shall assess aggregate authority enabled by related material rather than evaluating each cryptographic object in isolation. Classification and control design shall consider the maximum assets, validators, contract roles, governance powers, and recovery capabilities that related material can collectively reach. Changes that increase this aggregate authority shall be treated as material changes and approved before activation.

**17.2.2** This assessment shall cover seeds, mnemonics, HD roots, derived keys, Wallets, addresses, threshold or MPC shares, recovery arrangements, controlled assets, and protocol authorities. It shall identify parent-child derivation, quorum combinations, common administrators, shared providers, hidden single points of control, and paths by which one compromise could affect multiple authorities. The resulting relationships and risk decisions shall be reflected in the authoritative inventory and reviewed when assets, roles, membership, derivation scope, or recovery design changes.

### 17.3 Validator Safety

**17.3.1** Validator signing authority shall be bound to its intended network, validator identity, and signing domain. The Company shall prevent unsafe or conflicting signing during normal operation, failover, recovery, and migration. Binding and conflict-prevention controls shall operate at the signer or an equivalently trusted boundary and shall not depend solely on an upstream validator client.

**17.3.2** Where a protocol penalizes conflicting signatures, the Company shall permit only one active signing authority for a validator and network unless an approved design provides equivalent prevention of slashable or otherwise conflicting signatures. High availability shall not be achieved through independently active copies that lack coordinated protocol safety state and activation fencing. The design shall document applicable conflict conditions, failure and partition behavior, state coordination, fencing, tests, and accountable approval of residual penalty risk.

**17.3.3** Slashing-protection or equivalent protocol safety state shall be durable, integrity-protected, and monotonic where the protocol requires those properties. Before activating a replacement signer, the Company shall establish that no prior signing state exists or import and verify all safety state required by the protocol, and it shall fence or deactivate the predecessor. The Validator Key Management Standard shall define protocol-specific state, transfer, validation, and evidence requirements.

**17.3.4** If signer state, network identity, or prior signing safety is uncertain, the Company shall fail closed and escalate the event. The Validator Key Management Standard shall define protocol-specific client and operational requirements, safe-resumption criteria, evidence, and responsibility for assessing penalty exposure. The Company shall review any missed duties or conflicting-signature risk caused by the shutdown.

### 17.4 Protocol and Asset-Control Authority

**17.4.1** Treasury, withdrawal, governance, contract deployment, contract upgrade, and comparable protocol-control authorities shall satisfy Clauses 10.6 and 10.7 and use transaction authorization controls proportionate to classification and authority type. Web3-specific validation shall cover chain-specific payload encoding, contract parameters, governance or role changes, and intended on-chain effects. Material actions shall use independent approval and monitoring capable of detecting unexpected on-chain outcomes or authority expansion.

**17.4.2** Before authorization, the Company shall independently validate the intended network, destination, contract, role, asset, and authority change against authoritative information. After execution, it shall confirm and reconcile the resulting on-chain state against the approved target state. Predecessor authority shall remain monitored, restricted, or safely retained until transfer completion and rollback or recovery requirements have been resolved.

### 17.5 Threshold, MPC, Multisig, and Remote Signing

**17.5.1** Threshold, MPC, multisig, and remote-signing designs shall document custody, participants, quorum, independent trust domains, authorization, recovery, replacement, compromise, monitoring, lifecycle, and exit arrangements. Documentation shall identify where complete authority can be exercised or reconstructed, including coordinator services, administrative credentials, policy engines, backups, and provider-controlled recovery. Quorum and availability objectives shall be tested against participant loss, provider failure, network partition, malicious participation, and emergency operation.

**17.5.2** These mechanisms shall not be treated as interchangeable or as substitutes for accountable governance and transaction authorization. Selection shall consider whether control is enforced cryptographically, on-chain, operationally, or by a provider and shall identify the resulting trust, privacy, liveness, and compromise assumptions. A technically valid quorum shall not sign unless the required business authorization and final-payload controls have also succeeded.

**17.5.3** In addition to the organization-wide independence requirement in Clause 5.11, the Company shall assess common-control risks specific to MPC, multisig, and remote signing, including coordinator services, on-chain quorum changes, provider recovery paths, shared endpoints, and root administrators. A common dependency shall not defeat the intended quorum without explicit risk treatment. Residual aggregate-control risk shall receive approval proportionate to the highest authority enabled and shall have a time-bound remediation or exit plan.

### 17.6 Protocol-Mandated Cryptography and Lifecycle

**17.6.1** Protocol-mandated cryptography shall be governed under Clause 9.14. The inventory and Cryptographic Baseline shall additionally record the network, protocol version, key format, signing convention, affected authority, and applicable authority-transition method. The record shall identify any deviation from the Cryptographic Baseline and shall not characterize a protocol constraint as a direct NIST requirement.

**17.6.2** Protocol-specific treatment shall address authority transfer, validator exit, chain splits, asset migration, contract or governance transfer, provider dependency, and preservation of required evidence. Where no immediate migration is feasible, continued use shall be governed through the Cryptographic Baseline and, when required, a time-bound exception with documented monitoring and review outcomes.

## 18. References and Document Maintenance

NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev. 6 Initial Public Draft and NIST SP 800-131A Rev. 3 Initial Public Draft are monitoring and planning inputs only until final publication and formal adoption. **18.3** It shall use SP 800-130 for CKMS design, Part 3 for application guidance, and SP 800-131A for cryptographic transition. Each designation shall be supported by a concise rationale, and mixed-source controls shall identify the basis of each distinct element without overstating NIST coverage. A control classified as No clear basis shall be reviewed for necessity, correction, or removal rather than left unexplained.

**18.5** The mapping shall identify the source and revision, implementing document, owner, evidence, gaps, exceptions, compensating controls, and remediation. Evidence references shall be sufficiently specific to allow an independent reviewer to locate the current control design and a representative record of operation without exposing secret material. Mapping changes shall be version-controlled and traceable to their reviewer and approval.

**18.6** The Security Approval Authority shall review this Policy and its mapping at least annually and after a material incident. The review shall evaluate whether controls remain complete, correctly sourced, implemented, evidenced, and proportionate to current aggregate authority and risk. Its outcome shall record approvals, findings, accepted residual risk, required revisions, action owners, due dates, and closure criteria.

**18.7** It shall also review the Policy when relevant cryptographic publications, algorithms, protocol requirements, custody model, architecture, or operating model materially change. A material change includes one that alters protection boundaries, authority concentration, validator safety, recovery capability, provider dependence, cryptographic status, or the feasibility of migration. The Security Approval Authority shall determine whether interim controls, communication, training, or an exception is required before the next scheduled review.

**18.8** The review shall consider new, revised, superseded, withdrawn, or draft NIST publications and determine required updates. Draft material, including the initial public drafts identified in Clause 18.1, may inform planning, gap analysis, and transition preparation but shall not be represented as a final direct requirement or replace the adopted final revision without formal approval. The mapping shall record publication status and distinguish changes to NIST-derived controls from changes driven by protocols, Web3-specific risk, or Company governance.

**18.9** Revisions shall identify the reason for change, affected controls, approval, effective date, and required implementation, migration, communication, training, or exception actions. The controlled revision record shall also identify superseded content, accountable action owners, target dates, evidence of completion, and any deferred residual risk. Before closure, the Company shall verify that subordinate Processes, Standards, Procedures, inventories, mappings, and relevant third-party obligations are consistent with the approved revision.
